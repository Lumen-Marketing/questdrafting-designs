Eleven homepages, one place.

Every one uses the same real work: the studio's own renders, its 3D massing models and four sheets from a real permit set, with the sheet numbers printed on them. What you see on each card is that design's homepage, running live. Click a preview once to use it.

The first three are the chosen design built out into complete pages: one look, three different structures, and no two sections inside a page laid out the same way. The six after them are earlier alternative designs of the same page, each with its own typefaces, material and motion. The last two came first of all, and each runs its own section order.

A drafter works in overlays. The survey goes down first, the plan goes over it, the dimensions go over that, and every sheet in a set is drawn on the same origin so they land on each other exactly. This page is made of that: milky film laid on a graphite board, held by orange registration crosses. The opener prints the headline on a sheet of film laid over the render. The four services are strips of film across one plate, the work is a graphite mat with windows cut in it and the film panning behind them, the six steps are overlays each laid on the last, and the centre piece pins to the screen while the four sheets of one permit set land in register, one at a time, as you scroll.

Redline

What the studio actually sells is a permit. A redline is the marked up print that comes back from plan review, and answering those marks until the stamp issues is the whole service. So the page is a print with marks on it. Nothing is drawn on a render until you ask for it. The opener is a clean render carrying three numbered pins; tap one and it boxes the exact part of the building it refers to and prints the note. Same in the work section, at full size. The four services are clauses on a checklist, the permit set is a transmittal logging four sheets out, and the page closes on a submittal slip where choosing your project type writes the subject line of the email that leaves.
