Four homepages, four looks.

Every one uses the same real work: the studio's own renders, its 3D massing models and four sheets from a real permit set, with the sheet numbers printed on them. What you see on each card is that design's homepage, running live. Click a preview once to use it.

All four run the same sections in the same order, and no two of them lay a single section out the same way. Each one owns its own typefaces, its own material, its own way of building depth and its own piece of motion. So what you are choosing between is the design, nothing else.

A section cut through the house. A section is a real drawing: you cut the building open and draw what is inside. The opener does exactly that. A sun study is a real deliverable. The studio renders a house at dusk and at twilight so you can approve how it sits in the light, and this page does the same thing to itself: it starts warm and ends at night. In the opener you move the light by hand through three hours, and the render and the whole colour of the screen change with it. Then the six steps pin to the screen and travel sideways as you scroll. The work is a skyline of plates at different heights standing on one baseline, and the permit set is four sheets fanned out that straighten into a stack.

A drafter works in overlays. The survey goes down first, the plan goes over it, the dimensions go over that, and every sheet in a set is drawn on the same origin so they land on each other exactly. This page is made of that: milky film laid on a graphite board, held by orange registration crosses. The opener prints the headline on a sheet of film laid over the render. The four services are strips of film across one plate, the six steps are overlays each laid on the last, and the centre piece pins to the screen while the four sheets of one permit set land in register, one at a time, as you scroll.
